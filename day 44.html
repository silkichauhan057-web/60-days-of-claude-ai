Part 2

About section

Structure: Hook (before "see more") → Story → Proof → CTA. The first two lines are the only real estate that matters — they decide if anyone clicks further.

HOOK (LINE 1–2)

I didn't wait until I "knew enough" to start building — I learned SQL and Python by using them on a real Hospital Management System, not just a course project.

STORY (LINE 3–5)

I'm a data analytics student who cares more about whether an insight is useful than whether the code looks impressive. My toolkit is Python, SQL, Power BI, and Excel — and I use all four together, from cleaning messy data to building dashboards someone can actually act on.

PROOF (LINE 6–8)

Two projects I'd point you to: → A Hospital Management System — [add: what it tracked — patients, appointments, records? Built with SQL only, or SQL + Python?] → A data analytics project — [add: what dataset, what question you answered, what tool — Power BI or Python — you used to show the answer] What I bring beyond the tools: I pick up new concepts fast, and I stay calm when something breaks instead of panicking — which, in data work, is most days.

CTA (LAST LINE)

Recruiting for a data analyst or data internship? My projects are in my Featured section below — happy to walk you through the SQL or the dashboards. Let's connect.

Why this works

The hook doesn't say "aspiring data analyst" (everyone's profile says that) — it shows you already act like one. The proof section is the only part with real fill-ins, because I won't invent project details or numbers for you.

Why these 3 are pinned

SQL, Python, and Power BI are the three most-searched terms for entry-level data analyst roles. Pinned skills also show endorsements first — so this is where you want your strongest, most provable skills sitting.

Part 3

7-Day Activation Plan

A profile with no activity still doesn't get seen. This is what actually gets it in front of people.

1
Publish every profile change
›
  • Set headline (Option 1 to start)
  • Paste in the About section, with your real project details filled in
  • Add both projects (or set up an Experience/Projects section)
  • Add and reorder Skills, pin SQL / Python / Power BI
  • Upload a clear, front-facing profile photo
  • Add a banner image related to data/analytics (even a simple one helps)
  • Turn on "Open to Work" if you're targeting recruiters
2
Publish Post #1 — "I just built my LinkedIn"
›

A hook that invites comments, not just likes.

I just built my LinkedIn profile from scratch — as a data analytics student with zero "real" work experience yet. No fluff, no fake job titles. Just what I actually know: SQL, Python, Power BI, Excel — and two projects where I used them, including building a Hospital Management System from the ground up. Here's what I learned putting it together: your profile doesn't need a job title to be worth looking at. It needs proof you can do the thing. If you're a data analyst, recruiter, or fellow student — I'd genuinely love to connect and learn from how you got started. What's one thing you wish you knew when you were building your first project?
3
Send 10 strategic connection requests
›

Who to target: junior and senior data analysts at companies you'd want to work for, hiring managers/recruiters who post about data roles, alumni from your college now working in analytics, and people actively posting SQL/Power BI content.

Hi [Name], I'm a data analytics student building projects in SQL/Python/Power BI (just finished a Hospital Management System build). Would love to connect and learn from your path into analytics — thanks!
4
Comment on 5 posts in your target space
›

The Value Comment formula: Agree with a specific point → Add an insight from your own experience (even a small one, like something from your projects) → Ask a genuine question. This gets you seen by the poster and their audience — far more than a "Great post!" ever will.

5
Publish Post #2 — lessons from your project
›

A hot-take or lessons-learned post specifically about building the Hospital Management System — fill in the real detail before posting.

Building a Hospital Management System taught me something no tutorial did: [add your real lesson — e.g. "clean data structure matters more than clever queries" or "the hardest part wasn't the SQL, it was deciding what to even track"]. [Add 2-3 lines on what specifically went wrong or surprised you, and what you'd do differently next time.] If you're learning SQL right now — what's the one mistake you keep running into?
6
Engage + send 5 more connection requests
›

Reply to every comment on your posts within a few hours — this is what keeps LinkedIn's algorithm showing your post to more people. Then send 5 more targeted requests using the same template as Day 3, focused on a different company or niche this time.

7
Review your numbers
›

Profile views: a jump here means your headline/photo are getting clicked in search — good sign your keywords are working. Connection acceptance rate: under 30% usually means your request message needs to be more specific to the person, not more polished. Adjust the template in Day 3 based on which version got the best response, and repeat the loop next week.
