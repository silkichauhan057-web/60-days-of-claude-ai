How this was researched ▶

Each provider was rated 1–5 on every criterion by reviewing official pricing pages, product documentation, and public status pages listed in the Sources panel above. These are qualitative analyst ratings informed by real, named sources — they are not scraped live metrics, third-party benchmark scores, or published uptime percentages, and none of the underlying scores should be read as precise measured data.

Methodology

  • Pricing transparency was scored on how predictable a monthly bill is: flat/tiered plans score higher than granular, multi-dimensional usage pricing.
  • Ease of deployment / DX was scored on deploy friction: git-push/zero-config platforms score higher than platforms requiring manual infrastructure configuration.
  • Scalability & compute flexibility was scored on ceiling and control: raw IaaS with custom instance types scores higher than managed platforms with execution-time or resource caps.
  • Free tier generosity was scored on what a team can run in production-adjacent use for $0, and how likely that tier is to remain available.
  • Uptime & reliability was scored on the maturity of the platform's public reliability posture (published SLAs, historical incident transparency) — not on a measured uptime percentage, since Vercel, Render, and Railway do not publish historical uptime percentages for lower-tier plans.

Conflicts & caveats resolved during research

Free tier volatility (Render, Railway): Railway removed its general free tier in 2023 in favor of a one-time trial credit plus a paid Hobby plan; Render has adjusted free-tier limits and spin-down behavior for free web services more than once. Scores reflect the most recent known policy at time of writing — confirm current terms on each provider's pricing page before deciding.

No apples-to-apples uptime data: AWS publishes formal per-service SLAs (e.g., contractual uptime commitments with service credits). Vercel, Render, and Railway publish live/historical incident status pages but not a formal uptime SLA percentage on lower tiers. Rather than fabricate a comparable percentage for all four, uptime was scored on reliability posture and transparency instead of a single invented number.

"Ease of deployment" is inherently subjective: DX scores are grounded in each platform's documented deployment model (git-push, auto-detected build packs, container support) rather than a timed or user-tested benchmark, since no independent, citable DX benchmark study covering all four platforms exists.

Why AWS edges out Vercel despite a tied composite score

AWS and Vercel land on the same composite score (74/100) under these weights. The tie is broken in favor of AWS because it scores higher on the two heaviest-weighted criteria for a growing app — scalability and uptime — which matter more as usage and infrastructure complexity increase, even though Vercel offers a meaningfully better day-one developer experience.
